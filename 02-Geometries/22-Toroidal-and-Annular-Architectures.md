# Toroidal and Annular Concentrator Architectures

Toroidal non-imaging architectures provide an effective geometric configuration for stationary omnidirectional solar collection. By combining a toroidal reflector with a central vertical cylindrical absorber, the system achieves complete azimuthal invariance ($\Delta\phi = 360^\circ$).

## 1. Geometric Configuration

The architecture consists of:
1. **Central Vertical Cylindrical Receiver**: Radius $r_{\text{abs}}$, height $H_{\text{abs}}$, oriented along the $z$-axis.
2. **Annular Horizontal Aperture**: Spanning from inner radius $R_1$ to outer radius $R_2$.
3. **Toroidal Non-Imaging Reflector**: Generated by revolving a 2D tailored edge-ray reflector profile about the central $z$-axis.

```
          [ Sunlight Incident from Sky ]
            |       |       |       |
            v       v       v       v
         \                             /   <- Outer Reflector Lip (R2)
          \                           /
           \       +---------+       /
            \      | Central |      /      <- Toroidal Reflector Surface
             \     | Receiver|     /
              \____| (r_abs) |____/        <- Inner Reflector / Base
                   +---------+
```

## 2. 2D Profile Formulation for Cylindrical Absorbers

For a cylindrical absorber of radius $r_{\text{abs}}$, the 2D ideal non-imaging profile (Winston & Welford) consists of two continuous sections:

### Section 1: Involute of the Circle
From the bottom cusp ($\theta = 0$) up to the tangent release point ($\theta = \theta_a + \pi/2$):

$$r(\theta) = r_{\text{abs}} \sqrt{1 + \theta^2}$$
$$\phi(\theta) = \theta - \arctan(\theta)$$

The involute ensures all rays reflected from the reflector surface are directed onto the cylindrical receiver surface without re-entering the reflector cavity.

### Section 2: Parabolic Edge-Ray Section
Beyond the involute, the profile transitions into a parabola with its focus tangent to the cylinder and axis tilted at the design acceptance half-angle $\theta_a$:

$$r(\psi) = \frac{r_{\text{abs}} \left[ \psi + \theta_a + \pi/2 - \cos(\psi - \theta_a) \right]}{1 - \sin(\psi - \theta_a)}$$

## 3. Geometric Concentration Ratio

The effective aperture is an annular disk:
$$A_{\text{aperture}} = \pi \left( R_2^2 - R_1^2 \right)$$

The receiver is the circumferential area of the vertical cylinder:
$$A_{\text{receiver}} = 2\pi \, r_{\text{abs}} \, H_{\text{abs}}$$

The geometric concentration ratio is:
$$C_g = \frac{A_{\text{aperture}}}{A_{\text{receiver}}} = \frac{R_2^2 - R_1^2}{2 \, r_{\text{abs}} \, H_{\text{abs}}}$$

For an ideal 2D cylindrical collector with acceptance angle $\theta_a$:
$$C_{2D, \text{cylinder}} = \frac{1}{\sin\theta_a}$$

When revolved into a 3D toroid:
- Azimuthal symmetry ensures continuous operation as the sun sweeps from East to West.
- The illuminated flux band rotates smoothly around the cylindrical receiver perimeter, providing uniform daily thermal dissipation rather than local stationary hot spots.

## 4. Key Advantages for RoSoCol

1. **Uniform Heat Load**: Unlike flat receivers where peak flux produces severe central hot spots, the rotating focal region around the cylinder spreads thermal load circumferentially over diurnal transit.
2. **Low Profile**: Toroidal dishes have substantially lower aspect ratios ($H / D$) than single deep 3D paraboloids or tall 3D CPCs, drastically lowering wind loads.
3. **Fluid Loop Simplicity**: For solar thermal or hybrid PV/T applications, a vertical cylindrical pipe at the core simplifies fluid manifolding and eliminates flex hoses required by mechanical tracking systems.
