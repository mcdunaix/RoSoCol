# Foundational Bibliography: Non-Imaging Solar Optics

Annotated record of foundational texts, seminal papers, and theoretical treatments governing non-imaging concentration and rotational optics.

## 1. Classical Books and Monographs

1. **Welford, W. T., & Winston, R. (1989)**
   *High Collection Nonimaging Optics.* Academic Press, San Diego.
   - *Key Contribution*: Canonical text establishing Hamiltonian formulations of étendue, the edge-ray principle, 2D and 3D CPC derivations, and skew invariant analysis.
   - *Relevance to RoSoCol*: Chapter 4 (2D concentrators) and Chapter 5 (3D concentrators and skew ray limits).

2. **Chaves, J. (2015)**
   *Introduction to Nonimaging Optics (2nd Edition).* CRC Press.
   - *Key Contribution*: Accessible derivations of Cartesian ovals, Poisson brackets in optical phase space, and flow-line concentrator methods.

3. **O'Gallagher, J. J. (2008)**
   *Nonimaging Optics in Solar Energy.* Morgan & Claypool Publishers.
   - *Key Contribution*: Practical engineering methodologies for solar thermal and CPV applications, optical efficiency metrics, and secondary concentrators.

## 2. Seminal Journal Articles

1. **Winston, R. (1970)**
   *Light Collection within the Framework of Geometrical Optics.*
   *Journal of the Optical Society of America*, 60(2), 245–247.
   - *Key Contribution*: First mathematical formulation of the ideal 2D non-imaging collector approaching the $1/\sin\theta$ thermodynamic limit.

2. **Rabl, A. (1976)**
   *Comparison of Solar Concentrators.*
   *Solar Energy*, 18(2), 93–111.
   - *Key Contribution*: Rigorous mathematical comparison between imaging parabolic troughs/dishes and non-imaging CPCs, establishing the relationship between tracking precision, acceptance angle, and concentration ratio.

3. **Miñano, J. C. (1985)**
   *Design of Three-Dimensional Nonimaging Concentrators with Inhomogeneous Media.*
   *Journal of the Optical Society of America A*, 2(11), 1826–1831.
   - *Key Contribution*: Extended Poisson brackets and Hamiltonian flow to 3D phase-space tailoring; established limits for rotational symmetry without skew loss.

4. **Benítez, P., Miñano, J. C., & González, J. C. (2004)**
   *Simultaneous Multiple Surface (SMS) Optical Design Method in 3-D.*
   *Optical Engineering*, 43(7), 1489–1502.
   - *Key Contribution*: The SMS 3D method synthesizes two or more optical surfaces simultaneously to couple two input wavefronts to two output wavefronts, mitigating skew ray errors present in revolved 2D profiles.

5. **Welford, W. T., & Winston, R. (1980)**
   *Nonimaging Concentrators with Nonplanar Receivers.*
   *Optics Letters*, 5(5), 210–212.
   - *Key Contribution*: Derivation of involute reflector profiles wrapping around cylindrical and convex receivers; direct theoretical basis for [[22-Toroidal-and-Annular-Architectures]].
